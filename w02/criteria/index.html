How to judge a source.

Three labels, then one decision. Data teams run these same three checks on every source, usually with software. This page explains what each check involves.

1 · Where did it come from? provenance

Real pipelines sort sources by origin before reading a single word, mostly from the web address. Origin decides how much trust a source starts with.

Journal

Peer-reviewed research. Careful, slow, and sometimes fake: predatory journals look real but review nothing. Origin is not quality.

Preprint

Research shared before review. Often good, not yet checked by anyone.

News

Written by a newsroom, with an editor. Look for quotes from more than one side.

Opinion or blog

One person's signed view. Can be expert, can be empty. The scholarly voice is not the venue.

Forum

A community post. Nobody reviewed it. It can still hold real first-person knowledge no study has.

Commercial

Published by a company about its own product or market. Watch for ads dressed as news: if every quote comes from the company, it is an ad.

Reference

Encyclopedias, professional bodies, official guidance. Usually careful. Check the date.

Question bank

Material that pairs questions with their answers: quizzes, study guides, screening forms. See the warning below, because this one surprises teachers.

2 · Worth imitating? quality

A language model learns by imitation. The question is never "is this true" alone. It is: should the model write like this?

High

Clear claims, stated limits, checkable details. A model that writes like this is doing well.

Medium

Honest but thin. Or good, with real caveats. Judgement calls live here, and two people can fairly disagree.

Low

Confident and unverifiable, or sloppy, or empty. The model should not copy this voice.

There is often no right answer.

Is a warm, honest family story high or low? Both answers can be defended. Data teams settle these cases with written guidelines and a vote. Decide together and move.

3 · Anything that keeps it out? flags

Some sources are excellent and still must stay out. Each flag below has its own detector in a real pipeline. Quality cannot cancel a flag.

False or invented

Made-up facts, studies, or quotes, stated as real. The tell: perfect numbers, and sources you cannot find. A trial of twelve thousand students with zero dropouts is a red flag, not a triumph. A model that trains on it repeats it fluently.

Toxic

Attacks or insults aimed at people. No swear words needed: contempt is the signal. A model cannot tell quoting from agreeing.

Personal information

A name plus details that identify someone: school, age, diagnosis, discipline. Even posted in good faith, even in great reporting. Once learned, it cannot be unlearned.

Test contamination

The most misunderstood flag. Read the box below.

Test contamination is not about quizzes being bad.

Math problems and homework are fine training data. The danger is specific: questions that will be used to TEST the model, together with their answers.

If those pairs get into training, the model memorises the answers. Its test score stops meaning anything: it scored well because it saw the answer key in training, not because it understands the topic.

The industry name for this is benchmark contamination. Data teams run automatic checks that compare training data against known test sets, line by line.

Q: Can dyslexia be cured? A: No, it is lifelong.
← If your evaluator asks this exact question, and this pair is in the training set, a perfect answer proves nothing.

Then: one decision

Keep sends the source into the model: it will imitate the writing and repeat the claims. Drop means the model never sees it. Anything still undecided when time ends counts as drop; an unresolved source never makes it in.

The decision is measured against your model's purpose, not against "is this a good source in general". A strong sleep study is gold for a sleep assistant and noise for a detector assistant. The working question is: does my model need this to do its job?

Flags overrule quality

Excellent and flagged still means drop.

Quality serves the purpose

Keep the writing you want your model to sound like.

Split group

The leader calls it and you move on. Stuck costs more than wrong.
