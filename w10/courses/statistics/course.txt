SYLLABUS
============================================================
Course description
An introduction to the collection, organization, description, and analysis of data. Topics include sampling and experimental design, descriptive statistics, probability, discrete and continuous distributions, the Central Limit Theorem, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing for one and two samples, correlation and regression, and the chi-square test. Prerequisite: Intermediate Algebra or placement. Satisfies the general education quantitative reasoning requirement.

Instructor: Carol Whitfield, Adjunct Instructor · cwhitfield@eastvalley.example.edu
Office hours: Virtual office hours on Tuesdays 2:00-3:00 pm (Zoom link posted in the Week 1 module), or by appointment by email
Response time: Within 48 hours on weekdays

Learning outcomes
- Students will be able to describe and summarize data using appropriate tables, graphs, and numerical measures.
- Students will be able to apply basic rules of probability and work with discrete and normal probability distributions.
- Students will be able to construct and interpret confidence intervals for means and proportions.
- Students will be able to carry out and interpret hypothesis tests for one and two samples.
- Students will be able to describe the relationship between two numerical variables using correlation and regression.
- Students will be able to evaluate statistical claims found in everyday sources.

Course materials
- Illowsky, B., & Dean, S. (2023). Introductory statistics (2nd ed.). OpenStax. https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/1-introduction https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/1-introduction (Required. Free online; a printed copy is optional.)
