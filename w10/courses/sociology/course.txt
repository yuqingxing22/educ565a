SYLLABUS
============================================================
Course description
An introduction to the sociological study of society. Topics include sociological theory and research methods, culture, socialization, groups and organizations, deviance, social stratification, race and ethnicity, gender, family, education, and social change. Students learn to use the sociological perspective to analyze social issues. This course satisfies the university's general-education requirement in social and behavioral sciences.

Instructor: Dr. Rosalind Pettigrew, Associate Professor of Sociology · rpettigrew@calderstate.example
Office hours: Thursdays 2:00–3:00 pm ET by Zoom, first come first served; link on the Home page. Questions about grades and assignments should go to your TA first.
Response time: Within 48 hours on weekdays (TAs answer first)
Teaching Assistant (last names A–L): Marcus Hale · mhale@calderstate.example
Teaching Assistant (last names M–Z): Priya Venkataraman · pvenkataraman@calderstate.example

Learning outcomes
- Define and apply key sociological concepts such as the sociological imagination, culture, socialization, and stratification.
- Compare the functionalist, conflict, and symbolic interactionist perspectives and use them to analyze social issues.
- Describe the main methods sociologists use to study social life and evaluate basic research claims.
- Explain how social inequalities based on class, race, and gender are produced and maintained.
- Communicate sociological ideas clearly in writing and in online discussion.

Course materials
- Conerly, T. R., Holmes, K., & Lal Tamang, A. (2021). Introduction to sociology 3e. OpenStax. https://openstax.org/books/introduction-sociology-3e/pages/1-introduction https://openstax.org/books/introduction-sociology-3e/pages/1-introduction (Required; free online. A free PDF and low-cost print copy are also available from OpenStax.)

How a week works
Weeks open on Monday at 8:00 am ET. Read the assigned OpenStax sections and watch the two lecture videos early in the week. Initial discussion posts are due Wednesday at 11:59 pm ET, and two replies are due Sunday at 11:59 pm ET. Reading checks, in the weeks that have them, are due Sunday at 11:59 pm ET. There is no discussion in midterm week or finals week.

Grading
- Weekly discussions (14 × 10 points): 30%
- Reading checks (6 × 10 points): 15%
- Midterm exam: 25%
- Final essay: 30%
A 93–100, A- 90–92, B+ 87–89, B 83–86, B- 80–82, C+ 77–79, C 73–76, C- 70–72, D 60–69, F below 60.

Policies
Late work: Discussion posts submitted late lose 20% of the available points and are not accepted more than 48 hours after the deadline. Reading checks close at the deadline and are not reopened except for documented emergencies. The final essay loses 10% per day late.
Discussion participation: Each week you must write one initial post by Wednesday 11:59 pm ET and reply to two classmates by Sunday 11:59 pm ET. Posts should be respectful, use course concepts, and be written in clear, professional English. Posts that do not meet the length requirement may lose points.
Contacting the teaching team: Email your TA first. TAs will respond within 48 hours on weekdays. Emails sent on weekends will be answered the following Monday or Tuesday. Please check the syllabus and the announcements before emailing. The instructor handles grade appeals after you have contacted your TA.
Academic integrity: All work must be your own. Plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, and submitting work written by someone or something else without disclosure are violations of the University Academic Integrity Policy and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course.
Accessibility and accommodations: Students with documented disabilities should contact the Office of Accessibility Services at the start of the term so accommodations can be arranged. Lecture videos have captions. Transcripts are available on request.
Technology: You need a reliable internet connection and a recent browser. Technical problems are not an excuse for late work unless reported to the IT Help Desk with a ticket number before the deadline.
Use of AI tools: AI use must be disclosed. If you use generative AI tools in any work for this course, you must disclose that use to the instructor. Failure to disclose AI use will be treated as a violation of the University Academic Integrity Policy. Work you submit must be your own. If you are unsure about this policy, please contact your TA.
