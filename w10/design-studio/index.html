// Scenario

Your task: design one piece of an existing asynchronous course

The courses

Each topic is one online course that is already running. All four are fully asynchronous: learners work on their own schedule, with no live class meetings.

Your task

Design one piece of one course, so that learners feel the course is a community, using AI where it helps. The piece can be any size: part of one module (a game for one lesson), a whole week, or something that runs through the whole course (a course assistant). Keep it small enough to prototype in class.

The course sites

Each course is built as a mock course site in a learning management system (LMS), like Brightspace or Canvas. It gives you the basics: what the course teaches and how it runs now. Use it as background. You don’t have to use its content, and your design can change or add whatever it needs.

What each course site contains
  • Home and Announcements: what learners see first, and the instructor’s messages so far.
  • Syllabus: the schedule, policies and grading.
  • Modules: one page per week, every week complete, with readings, lecture videos and tasks.
  • Discussions, Quizzes (they check your answers) and People.

The institutions, instructors and classmates are fictional. The readings are real open resources. Lecture videos are listed by title but not included.

// How class runs

Main room and breakout rooms

Main room

Everyone works here, on their own prototype. Questions that don’t fit a room below can be asked here too.

Three breakout rooms, one per format. Go to your room when you have a question, or to work next to others making the same kind of thing. You are also welcome to visit any other room, just to see a format you are curious about. Come and go as you like; the instructors move between rooms. For a mixed format, such as a page with an AI assistant in it, choose the room for the hardest part.

Room 1

Pages

An AI writes the code: a week’s course page, a lesson, an interactive explanation.

Room 2

Games

An AI writes the code: a challenge, a scenario with choices, a quiz with feedback.

Room 3

AI assistants and skills

A course assistant the learner talks to (a GPT, or a chatbot built on an API), or a skill the learner adds to their own AI.

    Co-design Required

    What it is

    Designing with the learner, not only for them. While the design can still change, you show them ideas, sketches or options and let their reactions shape it.

    Why it matters

    Learners notice what designers miss: when they would really open it, what they would skip, what feels like a real person and what feels automated. Finding that out before you build costs little.

    How

    In Learner Persona Studio, type Design with [Name]. In the same chat right after the interview, your learner remembers what they told you. Show your idea in plain words or paste a sketch, listen, ask follow-up questions. Type End design session when done.

    You stay the designer and the researcher. What the learner says is evidence, not instructions: they may ask for something that would not help them learn, or prefer what is familiar. Their needs and your judgment both count; write down what you changed because of them, and what you kept anyway, and why.

    “Here are two ways this could start: [A] or [B]. Which would you actually open first, and why?”

    “In this version, your instructor drafts feedback with AI and then edits it. Would you want to know that? How would you want to find out?”

    Two examples only. Ask in your own words, about whatever you need to find out.

    Step 5 · Share your prototype and comment on a classmate’s

    Discussion board · after class
    • Post your prototype (a link, a file or screenshots) and your design log to the discussion board.
    • Make it easy to look at. Feedback is only as good as what people can find:
      • say where to start and what each part is;
      • say what is finished and what is a sketch;
      • end with the one or two questions you want feedback on.
    • Comment on at least one classmate’s prototype. Answer their questions first, then name one thing that would work for their learner and one thing you would question.
